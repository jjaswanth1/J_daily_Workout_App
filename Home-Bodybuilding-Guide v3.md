## HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS (Read this first!)

Every exercise below is written as **"Sets x Reps"** — for example **"Squats 3x15."**

- **Reps** = how many times you do the movement in a row without stopping
- **Sets** = how many times you repeat that whole batch, resting in between

**Example — "Squats 3x15":**
1. Do 15 squats one after another → that's **Set 1** done
2. Rest 60-90 seconds
3. Do 15 more squats → **Set 2** done
4. Rest again
5. Do 15 more squats → **Set 3** done
6. You're finished with squats for the day (45 squats total, done in 3 rounds — NOT all 45 at once)

**Example — "Push-ups 3 sets" (no rep number given):**
- Do as many push-ups as you can with good form → Set 1
- Rest, then repeat → Set 2
- Rest, then repeat → Set 3
- (Since you're a beginner, the exact rep count doesn't matter yet — just do as many good-form reps as possible each set)

**Example — "Side plank 2x each side":**
- Hold the side plank on your right side once → Set 1 (right)
- Switch, hold on your left side once → Set 1 (left)
- Rest, then repeat the right + left one more time → Set 2

Every "3x10," "3x12," "2x12," etc. anywhere in this guide follows this exact same pattern: **first number = how many rounds (sets), second number = how many reps in each round.**

## Quick Answer: Will This Work?
Yes. Bodyweight training + slightly increased protein intake will visibly build shoulders, chest, arms, back, and abs over 3-6 months. Real "shape" (moderate bulk, defined muscle) shows up around month 4-6 once you add dumbbells. This isn't fast, but it's realistic and lasting.

## Daily Stretch Routine (Every Single Day — No Clashes)

**Honest note first:** at 24, growth plates are closed — no stretch adds real bone length. What these DO: decompress your spine (compressed all day from sitting/gravity), which can reveal up to ~1 inch of your real height currently hidden by compression + slouching, and improve posture long-term. Worth doing daily for that reason.

**Why it's safe every day:** stretching isn't resistance training — it doesn't need 48hr recovery like push-ups/pull-ups do. Do this regardless of which strength day it is.

**When:** 5-7 min right after waking (11:30-11:40 AM, before warm-up), optional repeat before bed.

- **Cobra Stretch** — lie face down, push chest up through hands, hips stay on floor, hold 20-30 sec. Decompresses lower spine.
- **Cat-Cow** — on hands and knees, alternate arching and rounding your back slowly, 10 reps. Mobilizes the full spine.
- **Hanging (Dead Hang)** — use your pull-up bar, just hang with arms straight, 20-30 sec x 2-3 sets. Decompresses spine under gentle traction, also builds grip strength for pull-ups.
- **Forward Fold** — standing, hinge at hips, let arms/head hang toward floor, hold 20-30 sec. Stretches hamstrings and lower back.
- **Standing Side Reach** — reach one arm overhead and lean sideways, hold 15 sec each side. Lengthens the sides of your torso.

## Exercise Library — What Each One Does

### PUSH-UPS
- **Muscles worked:** Chest, shoulders, triceps, core
- **End result:** Builds chest width/thickness and arm definition — the #1 exercise for a "filled out" upper body look
- **Form:** Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in a straight line head-to-heels, lower chest near floor, elbows ~45° from body, push back up
- **Beginner mod:** Knees on floor, or hands on a raised surface (bed/table) if too hard
- **Reference:** [Healthline Calisthenics Guide](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness-exercise/calisthenics)

### BODYWEIGHT SQUATS
- **Muscles worked:** Quads, glutes, hamstrings
- **End result:** Builds leg mass and improves overall posture/balance — legs matter for proportion even if you're focused on upper body
- **Form:** Feet shoulder-width, push hips back like sitting in a chair, thighs to parallel, drive through heels to stand, chest stays upright
- **Reference:** [BOXROX Basic Calisthenics](https://www.boxrox.com/5-basic-calisthenics-exercises-for-beginners/)

### PLANK
- **Muscles worked:** Deep core (abs, obliques, lower back stabilizers)
- **End result:** Flattens/tightens the stomach area, and a strong core is what makes push-ups, posture, and every other movement more effective
- **Form:** Forearms on floor, body straight from head to heels, squeeze glutes and core, don't let hips sag or pike up
- **Reference:** [Gymshark Calisthenics Basics](https://row.gymshark.com/blog/article/calisthenic-workouts-for-beginners)

### PULL-UPS (or NEGATIVES)
- **Muscles worked:** Lats (sides of back), biceps, upper back
- **End result:** Biggest driver of back width and the "V-taper" look — pairs with push-ups as your two most important mass-building moves
- **Form:** Overhand grip slightly wider than shoulders, pull chin over the bar, lower with control, avoid kipping/swinging
- **Beginner mod (Negatives):** Jump or step up to the top position (chin over bar), then lower yourself as slowly as possible (3-5 sec) — do this until you can do a full unassisted rep
- **Equipment needed:** A doorway pull-up bar (no-drill models are widely available)

### TABLE / DOORWAY ROWS
- **Muscles worked:** Upper back, lats, biceps
- **End result:** This is your **posture exercise** — back muscles pull your shoulders back and up, directly countering the slouch from sitting/riding all day. Also builds the "V-taper" look people associate with a good physique
- **Form:** Lie under a sturdy table, grip the edge, feet flat on floor, pull your chest up to the table edge keeping body straight, lower slowly
- **No table sturdy enough?** Use a doorframe and towel wrapped around the frame, or lean back holding two door handles and row your body up

### GLUTE BRIDGES
- **Muscles worked:** Glutes, lower back, hamstrings
- **End result:** Supports lower back health and posture, balances out all the front-body pushing work
- **Form:** Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat, push hips up until body forms a straight line knee-to-shoulder, squeeze glutes at top, lower slowly

### SUPERMAN HOLDS
- **Muscles worked:** Lower back, glutes
- **End result:** Directly fixes forward-slouch posture and strengthens the spine — important since you already deal with neck/sleep-related tension
- **Form:** Lie face down, lift arms and legs off the ground simultaneously, hold 2-3 sec, lower with control

### WALL ANGELS
- **Muscles worked:** Upper back, shoulder mobility
- **End result:** Posture-correction specifically for shoulders/neck — do this daily, it's low-effort and high pay-off for your height and confidence goal
- **Form:** Back flat against a wall, arms in "goalpost" position touching wall, slide arms up and down without losing wall contact

### SKIPPING (JUMP ROPE)
- **Muscles worked:** Calves, shoulders (rope control), cardiovascular system
- **End result:** Keeps you lean, improves coordination and stamina, helps push-ups/squats feel easier by improving overall fitness
- **Form:** Rotate the rope with wrists, not shoulders, jump only 1-2 inches off ground, land softly on balls of feet
- **Reference:** [Healthline — Jump Rope Form](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness-exercise/calisthenics)

### CRUNCHES — *(Added now)*
- **Muscles worked:** Upper abs
- **End result:** Simpler, lower-demand ab isolation than plank — good core volume without extra fatigue
- **Form:** Lie on back, knees bent, hands lightly behind head, lift shoulder blades off floor using abs (not neck), lower slowly

### WALL SIT — *(Added now)*
- **Muscles worked:** Quads (isometric/endurance)
- **End result:** Builds leg endurance without impact — great finisher after squats
- **Form:** Back flat against wall, slide down until thighs are parallel to floor (knees at 90°), hold

### MOUNTAIN CLIMBERS — *(Added now)*
- **Muscles worked:** Core, shoulders, hip flexors + cardio
- **End result:** Dynamic full-body move that raises heart rate — doubles as a mini cardio burst
- **Form:** Start in plank position, drive knees toward chest alternately at a quick pace, keep hips level

### LUNGES — *(Phase 2 — Week 5+)*
- **Muscles worked:** Quads, glutes, hamstrings (single-leg)
- **End result:** Builds leg strength unilaterally — improves balance/symmetry beyond what squats alone give
- **Form:** Step forward, lower back knee toward floor, front knee stays over ankle, push back to start

### BICYCLE CRUNCHES — *(Phase 2 — Week 5+)*
- **Muscles worked:** Obliques + abs, rotational core
- **End result:** More dynamic than plank, hits the "sides" of your core for definition
- **Form:** Lie on back, hands behind head, bring opposite elbow to opposite knee in a pedaling motion

### RUSSIAN TWISTS — *(Phase 2 — Week 5+)*
- **Muscles worked:** Obliques, rotational core
- **End result:** Builds core rotation strength — also helps posture for guitar practice
- **Form:** Sit with knees bent, lean back slightly, twist torso side to side, feet can stay on floor as a beginner

### REVERSE GRIP PUSH-UPS — *(Phase 2 — Week 5+)*
- **Muscles worked:** Triceps, lower chest
- **End result:** Underhand grip shifts more work to triceps — a push-up variation for Phase 2
- **Form:** Same as push-ups but palms face forward (fingers pointing toward feet)

## Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Foundation — Form Over Everything
Don't worry about high reps. Focus entirely on correct movement.

**MONDAY & FRIDAY — Push + Legs + Core:**
- Push-ups: 3 sets, as many good-form reps as possible
- Bodyweight squats: 3 x 15
- Wall sit: 3 x 20-30 sec hold (finisher, right after squats)
- Plank: 3 x max hold
- Wall angels: 2 x 12

**WEDNESDAY — Pull + Posture + Core:**
- Pull-ups (or negatives): 3 sets, as many good-form reps as possible (or 3 slow negatives if no full rep yet)
- Table rows: 3 x 10
- Glute bridges: 3 x 15
- Superman holds: 3 x 12
- Side plank: 2 x max hold (each side)

**TUESDAY, THURSDAY, SATURDAY — Skipping + Core Finisher:**
- Skipping: 10 min continuous or interval (30 sec skip / 30 sec rest)
- Crunches: 2 x 15 (finisher, after skipping)
- Mountain climbers: 2 x 20 sec

**SUNDAY — Rest.** No training.

Rest 60-90 sec between sets. Full body warm-up before every session (jumping jacks, arm circles, bodyweight squats x10).

---

## Phase 2 (Weeks 5–10): Progression
Same weekly schedule as Phase 1 (Mon/Wed/Fri strength, Tue/Thu/Sat skipping, Sun rest) — only the exercises get harder:

- **Monday & Friday (Push + Legs):** move to full push-ups if you were on knees, then try decline push-ups (feet on a chair) — alternate with reverse grip push-ups some sessions for extra triceps work; add jump squats or Bulgarian split squats (back foot on a chair); alternate squats with lunges some weeks for single-leg strength; wall sit continues as finisher
- **Wednesday (Pull + Posture):** once negatives feel easy, attempt full unassisted pull-ups; slow the row tempo down (3 sec down) to increase difficulty; add pike push-ups (targets shoulders directly — builds that "capped shoulder" look)
- **Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday:** increase skipping to 15-20 min; rotate the core finisher between crunches, mountain climbers, bicycle crunches, and Russian twists (pick 2 per session, not all 4)
- If energy allows, you can add a short **Sunday** light session (just wall angels + plank, 10 min) — but Sunday can also stay a full rest day, your choice

---

## Phase 3 (Month 3-4+): Add Resistance
Once bodyweight feels too easy for 15+ reps:
- Buy adjustable dumbbells (5-20 kg range covers you for a long time)
- Add: dumbbell rows, shoulder press, bicep curls, goblet squats, Romanian deadlifts
- This is where visible "bulk" and shape really accelerate — bodyweight alone plateaus muscle growth around this point without added resistance
